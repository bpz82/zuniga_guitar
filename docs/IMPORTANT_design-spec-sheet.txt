### Two operating systems for building things that work

## PART I — THE ALGORITHM (Elon Musk)
**Order is non-negotiable. He says he's personally gone backwards on all five steps, multiple
times.**

**1. Make your requirements less dumb.**
Every requirement arrives with the name of a *person*. Never "the legal department." Never
"the safety department." A name. Then question it — no matter how smart that person is. His
own words: requirements from smart people are the most dangerous, because people are less
likely to question them. Question them even if they came from him.

**2. Suggest to Delete the part or process.**
NOT suggest to trim — suggest to delete. Calibration test: if you don't end up adding back at
least **10%** of what you deleted, you didn't delete enough. Tell the team in advance you're
going to over-delete on purpose. DO NOT DELETE ANYTHING WITHOUT ASKING
BLAKE FIRST.

**3. Simplify and optimize.**
Only now. The common mistake is simplifying a part or process that should not exist at all.

**4. Accelerate cycle time.**
Every process can be sped up — but only after the first three. His line: if you're digging your
grave, don't dig it faster. Stop digging.

**5. Automate.**
Last. Always last. The Nevada and Fremont failure was starting here.

### The corollaries (the postscript)
- **Camaraderie is dangerous.** It makes it hard for people to challenge each other's work.
- It's OK to be wrong. Just don't be confident and wrong.
- Never ask your troops to do something you're not willing to do.
- Don't just meet with managers. **Skip a level.**
- **A maniacal sense of urgency is our operating principle.**
- *The only rules are the ones dictated by the laws of physics. Everything else is a
recommendation.*

### Three mechanisms that make the algorithm run

**Walk to the red.** A monitor shows every station green or red. Walk straight to the red. Ask
why until you hit root cause.

**The idiot index.** A $120,000 quoted part was no more complicated than a garage door
opener — built in-house for $5,000. A $1,500 NASA latch replaced by a modified
bathroom-stall latch for $30. A $3M air cooling system solved with modified commercial AC
units.

# ADDENDUM — other design principles worth utilizing

### On simplicity
Simplicity improves reliability *and* reduces cost — fewer components means fewer to buy and
fewer that can go wrong. And it doesn't arrive in one stroke: simplicity comes from hundreds of
little changes. **genius has the fewest moving parts.**

### On complexity and scale
Complexity is the enemy of scaling.
### On design
Design is the fundamental soul of a creation that ends up expressing itself in successive outer
layers."*

### On deliberate delight
The Model S door handles sit flush, then light up and pop out as you approach — *a happy
handshake.* Everyone said a normal handle would work just as well. He took it anyway: surprise
creates emotional bond, and people tell other people. Showmanship is salesmanship.
### On evolution over prediction
*Create an environment that fosters innovation and let it evolve in a Darwinian way. You don't
want to pick one technology or path and decide it will win, because it may not be the best
option.* Failure is a side effect of iteration. His test for an engineer: **if you can't tell me four
ways you screwed something up before you got it right, you weren't the one doing the real
work.**
### On repetition as a design tool
**if your subordinates don't already know what you're about to say, you're not saying it
enough.**
### On the limits of any framework
*there is no one good way to build all airplanes.*
### Kelly's three underrated points
**Integrity is a performance multiplier.*
**Mistakes are acceptable; concealed mistakes are not.** They had to be reported immediately,
with recommendations for correction.
**Know when to kill your own brilliant idea.*
